## Key Driver

**Under-dispatch during high-price intervals (missed discharge opportunities)**

During the top 10 highest-price intervals, historical dispatch missed the perfect counterfactual in 5 of 10 cases. The largest single-interval gaps occurred when historical operation **charged or idled while perfect foresight discharged**:

## Secondary Contributing Factor

**Low state-of-charge during high-price windows**

Among intervals above the 75th-percentile price threshold ($64.47/MWh), 75 intervals had historical under-dispatch, contributing **$64,738** in gap. Within that set, **7 intervals** had SOC below 30% during high prices, adding **$2,977** in gap — indicating that insufficient pre-charging before peak periods limited discharge capability.

Average SOC during high-price intervals was 58.0% vs 50.8% overall, but the low-SOC tail during peaks prevented full capture of spike revenue.

**Evidence:** `analyze_state_of_charge_patterns()`.

## Recommendations

### 1. Pre-position SOC before known peak windows

- **Action:** Raise minimum SOC target to ~60% by 11:00 AM on weekdays, ahead of the recurring afternoon price peak (12:00–15:00).
- **Rationale:** The largest gap intervals show the battery charging or sitting at low/negative dispatch during $69–80/MWh windows. Ensuring adequate SOC before peaks would allow fuller discharge when prices spike.
- **Expected benefit:** Closing even 30% of the top-10 interval gap (~$5,400/week) without changing bidding logic.
- **Tradeoff:** Higher pre-peak SOC reduces headroom for regulation/ancillary services and may forgo some low-price charging opportunities earlier in the morning.

### 2. Add a price-triggered discharge floor during top-quartile prices

- **Action:** When spot price exceeds $64/MWh (75th percentile), enforce a minimum discharge target proportional to available SOC (e.g., at least 50% of nameplate power if SOC > 40%).
- **Rationale:** 75 of 168 high-price intervals showed under-dispatch vs perfect foresight. A hard floor prevents the battery from charging or idling during the most valuable intervals.
- **Expected benefit:** Directly addresses the primary driver; even a 20% reduction in high-price under-dispatch gap (~$13,000/week) is achievable.
- **Tradeoff:** Rule-based floors can conflict with market-specific bidding constraints and may increase cycling/degradation if triggered too frequently.
